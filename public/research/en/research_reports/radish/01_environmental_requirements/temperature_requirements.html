Radish (Daikon) — Temperature Requirements by Growth Stage ​

Overview ​

Radish (daikon) is a cool-season root crop whose cycle includes germination, seedling (leaf growth), root enlargement, and flowering (bolting). Appropriate temperature at each stage is critical; departure from optimal ranges risks poor germination, root quality loss, bolting, and pollen sterility. Using authoritative Japanese and academic sources, this report tabulates optimal_min, optimal_max, base_temperature, low_stress_threshold, high_stress_threshold, max_temperature, frost_threshold, and sterility_risk_threshold (flowering only) by stage. Cultivar and regional differences are noted where documented; otherwise general values are given.

Parameter Definitions and Rationale ​

  • optimal_min / optimal_max: Germination is best at 18–24°C; subsequent growth and root enlargement at 10–18°C. Germination rate and uniformity decline above 25°C[1][2].
  • base_temperature: 4–5°C for germination and growth; below this, growth stops or germination fails[3][4].
  • low_stress_threshold / frost_threshold: Seedlings and roots tolerate to about −4°C; below that, injury or death occurs. Harvest before hard frost (−4°C or below) is recommended[2][3][5].
  • high_stress_threshold / max_temperature: Above 29°C, growth is poor and bolting advances; 29°C is a practical upper limit[2][3].
  • sterility_risk_threshold: No radish-specific data; related brassicas suggest increased pollen sterility and poor seed set above 30–32°C (estimate)[6].
  • Cultivar/regional differences: Heat-tolerant and slow-bolting types are recommended for warm/tropical systems; specific thresholds are often in catalogs and trials[3][2].

Detailed Stage Descriptions ​

Germination ​

  • Optimal 18–24°C; germination possible near 5°C but slower at low temperature. Above 25°C, germination rate falls.
  • ~4°C is effectively the base temperature; extreme cold below 4°C risks injury though germination cold tolerance is relatively high.

Seedling / Leaf Growth ​

  • Optimal growth 10–18°C; above 29°C, root enlargement is poor and immature plants bolt easily.
  • Tolerance to frost to about −4°C; below that, plants die or stop growing.
  • Growth likely begins around 5°C.

Root Enlargement ​

  • Ideal 10–18°C, similar to leaf growth; heat and cold stress need more attention than in leaf stage; above 29°C enlargement is inhibited and quality drops.
  • Hard frost (−4°C or below) can damage roots—harvest before severe frost.
  • Peak enlargement and harvest timing vary slightly by cultivar.

Flowering (Bolting) ​

  • Similar optimal and heat-stress conditions to root enlargement; above 29°C, bolting and flowering are promoted.
  • Related brassicas: above 30–32°C, pollination failure and poor seed set are possible; radish-specific values are not stated.
  • Flower initiation and sterility risk vary by cultivar and region—consult local guides and seed catalogs.

Cultivar and Regional Differences ​

  • In Japan, cultivars differ in heat tolerance and bolting resistance; early vs. late types and heat tolerance affect success in warm regions.
  • Specific thresholds may appear in catalogs and trials—refer to local extension materials when selecting cultivars.
