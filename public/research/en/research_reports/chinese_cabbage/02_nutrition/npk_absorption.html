Chinese Cabbage — NPK Absorption (g/m²) by Growth Stage ​

1. Introduction ​

Chinese cabbage (Brassica rapa) is a major leafy vegetable in Japan and Asia. Adequate nutrients at each stage are essential for high yield. This report summarizes NPK uptake (g/m²) at seedling, rosette, folding, heading, and flowering stages from literature, extension guides, and WOFOST model results.

2. Growth Stages and Nutrient Uptake ​

2.1 Stage Definitions ​

  • Seedling (germination to ~5 true leaves)
  • Rosette (horizontal outer leaves, plant establishment)
  • Folding (inner leaves rise; initial head formation)
  • Heading (dense head development)
  • Flowering (flower initiation to seed)

Full cycle is typically 80–100 days in standard field production[1][2][3][4][5].

2.2 Temporal Uptake Pattern ​

Potassium uptake is highest, followed by N then P. Peak uptake occurs from rosette through early heading; rate declines toward flowering[6][7][8].

3. Stage-wise NPK Data (Measured and Estimated) ​

3.1 Direct Data Availability ​

Direct stage-wise N, P, K (g/m²) measurements are very limited; most sources give total uptake and stage percentages[6][8][9][10]. Allocation from totals is standard.

3.2 Estimation Method ​

  • Total uptake example (4–5 kg/m² yield): N 30–35 g/m²; P 6–7 g/m²; K 40–45 g/m²[10]
  • Stage shares (standard domestic model[8][9][10]): Seedling 10–15%; Rosette 30–35%; Folding 25–30%; Heading 15–20%; Flowering 5–10%

WOFOST validation shows similar sigmoid curves (rRMSE ~0.15)[9][11].

4. Usage Notes ​

  • Adjust for cultivar, season, weather, and fertilization; use local extension data where available[6][8][10].
  • Fertilizer recovery efficiency (N ~30–40%, P ~10%, K ~40–50%) means applied rates exceed uptake[6].
  • Values are whole-plant uptake; not all nutrients accumulate in the edible head[8][10].

5. Conclusion ​

Chinese cabbage stage-wise NPK can be estimated as total uptake × established stage percentages—a method validated by WOFOST and widely used for brassicas. Fertilization through heading is most critical for market production.
