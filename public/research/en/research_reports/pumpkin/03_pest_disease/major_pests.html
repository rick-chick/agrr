Pumpkin (Cucurbita spp.) — Major Pests and Damage Symptoms Worldwide ​

Overview ​

Pumpkin (Cucurbita spp.) is an important crop worldwide, grown across diverse climates in Asia, the Americas, Europe, and Africa. Pest damage varies by region in species and severity, but symptoms include wilting, leaf and fruit feeding, internal infestation, and virus transmission. This review summarizes internationally recognized major pumpkin pests, characteristic damage, distribution, and importance, drawing on Japanese and English sources.

Major Pumpkin Pests and Damage Symptoms ​

1. Red Pumpkin Beetles (Aulacophora spp., Raphidopalpa foveicollis) ​

  • Distribution: Widespread in India and Asia; major pest as “uri hamushi” in Japan[1][2][3][4][5][6].
  • Damage: Adults and larvae feed on leaves, flowers, and fruit—holes and skeletonized leaves. Early growth stages suffer most, causing stunting and yield/quality loss[1][2][5].
  • Management: Crop rotation, trap crops, physical removal, insect netting[1][5].
  • Note: Among the most important pests in Asia; less critical in Europe and Africa.

2. Melon Fruit Flies (Bactrocera cucurbitae and others) ​

  • Distribution: Asia, Africa, tropics/subtropics; “pumpkin fruit fly” is important in Japan[1][2][3][4][6][7].
  • Damage: Females oviposit on fruit; larvae feed internally causing rot and drop. Pinholes and exudate on the surface[1][6][7].
  • Related species: B. depressa in Japan/Korea; Anastrepha grandis in South America (quarantine pest)[8][9].
  • Management: Fruit bagging, bait traps[6][7][8].
  • Note: Major pest in warm regions; severe for fresh consumption.

3. Aphids (Aphis gossypii and others) ​

  • Distribution: Nearly worldwide; among the most important pests[1][2][3][4][5][6][10][11].
  • Damage: Curling, yellowing, stunting; honeydew and sooty mold; major vectors of pumpkin mosaic and other viruses[5][10][11].
  • Management: Resistant cultivars, netting, rogueing (insecticides limited for virus control)[5][10][11].
  • Note: Key virus vectors; difficult to manage.

4. Whiteflies (Bemisia tabaci and others) ​

  • Distribution: Expanding in warm to tropical zones; increasingly important globally[5][11].
  • Damage: Yellowing, leaf discoloration and wilting, sooty mold; virus transmission (e.g. cucurbit yellow stunting disorder)[1][5][10][11].
  • Management: Rotation, row covers; widespread insecticide resistance[5][11].

5. Cucumber Beetles (North America) ​

  • Distribution: Major in North America; Aulacophora spp. play a similar role in Asia[5][12].
  • Damage: Leaf, flower, and root feeding; skeletonization; bacterial wilt vector[12].
  • Management: Barriers, rotation, early-season sprays[5][12].

6. Pickleworms (Diaphania hyalinata, D. indica) ​

  • Distribution: Major in the Americas; introduced cases in Japan[13].
  • Damage: Larvae bore into leaves, stems, buds, and fruit; up to ~80% yield loss; leaves webbed with silk[13].
  • Management: Remove infested leaves; Bt and other biopesticides[13].

7. Squash Vine Borer (Melittia cucurbitae) ​

  • Distribution: Most important in North America; rare reports in Asia[12][14].
  • Damage: Larvae bore stems and vines causing sudden wilt and death[14].

8. Squash Bug (Anasa tristis) ​

  • Distribution: North to Central America and parts of South America[12][15].
  • Damage: Sap feeding causes yellow-brown spots, wilt, death; yellow vine disease vector[15].
  • Management: Physical removal of egg masses and nymphs[15].

9. Leaf Miners (Liriomyza spp.) ​

  • Distribution: Important in Asia, South America, and the US[1][2][3][5][11][16].
  • Damage: Larval tunnels reduce photosynthesis; cumulative damage causes poor growth[16].
  • Management: Biopesticides, natural enemies, remove damaged leaves[11].

10. Spider Mites (Tetranychus urticae) ​

  • Distribution: Worldwide; outbreaks in hot, dry periods[1][2][3][5][17].
  • Damage: Yellow-white stippling, webbing, defoliation and yield loss in severe cases[17].

Regionally Important and Quarantine Pests ​

  • Anastrepha grandis (South American cucurbit fruit fly): Not in Japan but high quarantine risk on imported fruit; oviposition under skin, internal rot; heat treatment and systems approach recommended[9].
  • Bactrocera depressa: Important in Korea and Japan (population movement between countries reported)[8].

Pests as Virus Vectors ​

  • Aphids, whiteflies, and pumpkin beetles are major vectors of mosaic and potyviruses on pumpkin. Control combines resistant cultivars, netting, and rogueing—not vector eradication alone[5][10][11][12].

Important Pests in Japan (Practical Review) ​

Field guides rank pumpkin beetles, aphids, pumpkin fruit fly, and spider mites highly; physical control (netting, bagging), rotation, and understanding phenology are emphasized[5][6][7][8].

Conclusion ​

Pumpkin pest damage spans leaf and fruit feeding, wilt and death, internal fruit infestation, and virus spread. Although species importance varies by region, aphids, pumpkin beetles, fruit flies, whiteflies, and stink bugs are recognized worldwide. Integrated pest management (IPM), resistant cultivars, and insect netting are recommended.
